How the engine prices a bet

Every number below is reproducible from these steps
  1. Market. Convert each book’s odds to implied probability. Where a book prices both sides, remove its margin proportionally to get a no-vig probability, then average across books.
  2. Independent model. Take independent pregame model probabilities where published (currently SportRadar for NHL). Weight each source by 1/σ²: a single external model is treated as ±5 pts (1σ), a multi-book no-vig consensus as ±3 pts, a single book as ±4 pts.
  3. Adjustments. Add documented factors the market may not fully price (rest, travel, lineups, goaltending), each with its own size and uncertainty. Uncertainties add in quadrature.
  4. Interval. Report the mean probability with an 80% interval (±1.28σ).
  5. Expected value. EV = p × net profit − (1 − p) × stake, at the best available price, at the mean and at the interval’s low end.
  6. Rank. Rank score = EV per $100 at the low end × market liquidity (moneyline 1.0, totals and spreads 0.9, props 0.6, exotics 0.4). Only bets that stay positive at the low end are recommended.

Not done yet: a historical backtest of a sport-specific model. The engine is evaluated walk-forward on its own logged pregame probabilities instead (see Model reliability). No result is guaranteed; an 80% interval means the true probability falls outside it about one time in five.

    Jurisdiction

    Prices from any other book are shown for reference only

    Edges, EV, stakes, recommendations and the edge tape only use prices from sportsbooks licensed by iGaming Ontario / AGCO. Offshore books, US-only books and prediction markets are greyed out.

    List checked against the iGaming Ontario operator directory, Nov 2026. Prices quoted from US-facing sources for FanDuel and DraftKings can differ slightly from their Ontario apps, so confirm before betting.
